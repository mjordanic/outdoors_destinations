# SuggestedLoops: similar hikes composed from the TrailGraph

A Ranking lists Catalog DayHikes only, and the Catalog holds a hike only where an OSM relation draws one. Where the marked network is dense but the relations are few or are lines, the best match for a Seed is often a loop no relation draws: the research behind ADR-0018 found composed loops scoring above the best Catalog DayHike in both test areas. A Ranking now lists such loops beside the DayHikes. A composed Seed (ADR-0018) gets them too.

**Search at query time, seeded by the Catalog.** Every combination of Segments cannot be stored: the marked network holds 10^7 to 10^13 loops of 20 km or less in Europe, nearly all near-copies. So nothing is precomputed. A Ranking ranks the Catalog as before (with the DriveTime pre-filter), then takes the Trailheads of its best 30 DayHikes, in rank order: similar terrain and climate cluster there. Near each Trailhead it takes the three nearest Junctions within 2 km where two trail Segments meet, at most 40 in all. With the DriveTime filter on, the drive time to each of those Junctions is asked too, and one out of reach is dropped.

**A beam that follows the Seed's ElevationProfile.** From each start Junction, a beam search grows walks over trail and gap Segments (never a Link, which the Hiker crosses only by choice; a WayBack never crosses one either). It keeps the 64 walks whose heights so far are closest to the Seed's ElevationProfile, stretched to the Seed's length, highest and lowest point, walked either way round. A walk visits no Junction twice. It closes only back at its start, by a Segment other than the one it left on. A Dijkstra from the start drops a walk whose way home would make it longer than the band. The band is 0.75 to 1.25 times the Seed's length, at least 1 km, and at most 40 km (the longest DayHike there and back). Each completed loop gets a cheap guess at the core Measures on elevation and length. Of loops that share a Twin's share of their Segments' length, only the better guess stays.

**Measured and scored like a Seed, then merged.** The best 40 guesses are stitched from their Segments (`TrailGraph.walk_segments`) and measured with the same functions as a ComposedHike (ADR-0018). With ReturnToStart they include the WayBack, as every hike in that mode does (ADR-0019); a loop ends at its start, so its WayBack is none. They are scored against the Seed with the Ranking's weights, QueryMonth and Calibration, beside the memory-mapped store, as a composed Seed is. Then, best first, a loop is left out when more than half of it lies on the Seed's line or more than half of the Seed's line lies on it (it mostly walks the Seed again; "on" is within 30 m, as for Twins), or when it is a Twin of any Catalog DayHike (on the served lines, as a composed Seed's retraced DayHikes are).

**One row per area, at most three versions (the captain's rule).** Loops cluster: from one area the beam closes many loops that differ by a detour, and a list of near-copies helps no one (9 of 10 rows were composed loops of one wine route for one Seed). So a loop starting within 10 km of the start of a better loop already kept is a version of it, not a row of its own. A row holds at most three versions, its own loop included, and a loop is a version only when more than half of its length lies off every version already kept; otherwise it is left out. The rows merge with the Catalog results by their best loop's score, and the best 10 are listed. Catalog DayHikes are listed as before; the rule is for composed loops only.

**Named by where it starts.** A SuggestedLoop is named after the named amenity point nearest its start: "Loop from" it within 500 m, "Loop near" it within 2 km, else by its start's coordinates. A name from the trails walked longest, as a drawn ComposedHike proposes, would borrow the name of a Catalog DayHike the loop overlaps.

**A suggestion is a ComposedHike.** A SuggestedLoop is listed with `id` null, its `route` (the text of its shared link, ADR-0018), its simplified line, its name and the figures a DayHike row has, and its row's other versions in `versions`. With the DriveTime filter on, it also has the hours to its start. The page marks a row "composed", says how many versions it has, and draws it dashed green on the "Select hike on map" map. Clicking the row lists its versions under it as var 1, 2 and 3 and draws them all on the map; each opens through `POST /api/compose` with its name and link. "Edit on the map" makes it the liked hike and opens it in the composer, to go on drawing from it.

**Bounded per request.** The search runs inside the one-Ranking-at-a-time limit of the web app. One Ranking reads at most 60,000 Segments (keeping 8 heights of each), tries at most 400,000 walk extensions, and stops all loop work, measuring included, after 5 s with what it has. On the Slovenia export (1,672 DayHikes, 10,480 Segments, 8-core Mac, one process), 30 random Seeds take 0.24 s median, 0.58 s at p90 and 0.88 s at most with ReturnToStart (0.09 s, 0.25 s and 0.69 s one way), against 4 ms for the Catalog alone; the busiest search used 275,000 of its 400,000 extensions. The measuring process peaks at 0.31 GB. With ReturnToStart a list holds 1.8 composed rows on average (median 2, at most 4, none for 2 of the 30 Seeds), with 2.2 versions per row; a composed row tops the list for 7 Seeds. One way, where most Seeds are lines and loops are measured against them as drawn, a list holds 0.6 composed rows on average (none for 20 Seeds).

**Considered Options**: a bounded precompute of distinct loops per Trailhead as extra store rows (0.5 to 4 million rows in Europe, about 50 CPU-hours a build, Rankings 5 to 10 s unfiltered, and a loop that would match well may be clustered away behind a worse near-copy); exhaustive enumeration with a cheap pre-score (does not finish in dense areas, and scored worse than the beam where it did); numba to compile the beam (the beam's steps are numpy over all walks of a level, already fast enough, without a JIT warm-up or another package in the image); starting from every Junction near the Seed rather than near the best results (terrain like the Seed's is where the best results are); composed lines (A to B) now (10^3 to 10^5 times more of them than loops; a later change); listing every loop that is no Twin of a better one (near-copies that differ by a detour filled the list); naming a loop by its trails (it borrowed a Catalog DayHike's name). Rejected.

**Consequences**: a Ranking from an export with a TrailGraph costs about 0.1–1 s more and, with the DriveTime filter, one more drive-time request. The CLI `dayhike compose` lists a SuggestedLoop by its link text in place of an id; `dayhike rank` (on the Catalog, not a serving export) is unchanged. The same loop walked from two starts has two ProfileShapes and may score differently; the better is kept. Within 10 km only one composed row is listed, so a second good loop there shows only as a version.
