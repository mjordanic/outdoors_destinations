# TrailGraph sidecar and ComposedHikes

A Hiker who picked a DayHike on the map often likes most of it but not all: a variant, a shortcut, a short loop that is not in the Catalog. They can now draw their own walk from the marked hiking network and rank Catalog DayHikes against it. That needs a graph of the whole marked network, which the Catalog and the GeometryStore do not hold: they keep the main walk of each surviving DayHike, not the relations ingest dropped, and not how the ways of different relations meet.

**The TrailGraph is a sidecar written from the work file.** `build_catalog` writes `{catalog}.trailgraph.sqlite` from the ingest work file just before it deletes it, so there is no new OSM pass. Its input is every member way of every `route=hiking` relation ingest read, including relations dropped as too short, too long, superroutes or node-network legs. Unmarked paths stay out, and so do short relations from the Catalog: the 1–20 km band holds, and a short relation appears only as Segments.

**Junctions and Segments.** Ways split at every node two of them share. Two same-layer ways that cross with no node get a Junction at the crossing. A Junction also sits where the set of relations along a trail changes at a node only two ways share, so a stage boundary is a Junction and a Catalog stage can be composed from its own Segments. Otherwise degree-2 chains merge, Junction to Junction. Each Segment keeps its OSM way and relation ids, metres and surface per way, strictest `sac_scale`, and full-resolution vertices (coordinates as OSM's 1e-7 degree integers, so a Segment's vertices are exactly the GeometryStore's), with an R*Tree over Segment boxes.

**Snapping.** A dead-end tip joins the nearest node of another way within the snap tolerance (`ingest --snap-tolerance`, 20 m by default, at most the 50 m `GAP_JOIN_M`) as a `gap` Segment, but only when that node is more than three times the tolerance away along the network, and never across a bridge, a tunnel or another layer. Two ways that pass within the tolerance, with no join nearer than 100 m along the network, are not joined: their closest node pair is offered as a Link ("connect here"), which the Hiker may cross as a straight step.

**Per-vertex samples.** `sample-trailgraph` stores per vertex what the Measures read from rasters: FABDEM height, WorldCover class, CHELSA cell (the cell's 12 months × 3 values once in a table), slope southness at the SolarAspect points, and the WorldCover overview cells beside open water near the network. A serving container has no rasters (ADR-0014), so this is what lets it compute a ComposedHike's facts.

**A ComposedHike is measured as drawn.** It is a start Junction and a list of Segment ids, each starting where the walk so far ends. It may reuse a Segment and counts its distance each time it is walked. Its facts come from the same functions as a DayHike's facts, on the vertices walked, and its amenity facts from the amenity points kept near the network, measured from its start. It is not stored: the URL `#compose=start:1,2,3` shares it.

**Ranking against a ComposedHike.** The serving export carries a TrailGraph subset (`trailgraph.sqlite`, export format 4, with each Segment's simplified line and climb each way). `GET /api/segments` serves the Segments in a map box; `POST /api/compose` returns the same detail shape as a Catalog hike; the ranking and pair endpoints and `dayhike compose` accept a composed Seed. A composed Seed is compared with Catalog DayHikes only. The DayHikes it retraces, its Twins on their served lines, join its Twin group and are not listed.

**Considered Options**: unmarked paths too (doubles the graph and invites walks no one maintains); a second OSM pass for the graph (the work file already has every way ingest read); auto-joining every pass-by within the tolerance (two trails on either side of a stream would merge; a Link lets the Hiker decide); keeping short relations as DayHikes (they would crowd the Catalog; as Segments they serve composing); precomputing combinations as candidate DayHikes and suggesting composed routes as similar hikes (a later change); storing ComposedHikes on the server (the URL is enough). Rejected.

**Consequences**: the Catalog must be rebuilt (`ingest`), then `sample-trailgraph`, then the serving export, to compose hikes; an export without a TrailGraph serves as before, without the "design your own" button. The TrailGraph for Slovenia is 35 MB beside the Catalog and 27 MB in the serving export for 0.75 M vertices, so a Europe image grows by about 1.5–2 GB. Composing a Catalog DayHike's own Segments reproduces its facts; on Slovenia 1,514 of 1,672 do exactly. Of the rest, 46 differ only in Difficulty or SurfaceMix (the Catalog counts a Leftover's `sac_scale`, and overlapping OSM ways credit surface differently), and 112 cannot be drawn Segment by Segment, because their main walk jumps a gap of up to 50 m inside the relation that the TrailGraph does not join, or steps over a repeated node the main walk dropped. WaterSide of a ComposedHike reads the stored cells, which reach as far as the 200 m column at the cell centre's latitude, so it can differ from a raster read at the edge of a cell. A crossing Junction adds a vertex the DayHike lacks, so a walk across it is a few centimetres longer.
