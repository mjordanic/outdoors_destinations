# TrailGraph sidecar and ComposedHikes

A Hiker who picked a DayHike on the map often likes most of it but not all: a variant, a shortcut, a short loop that is not in the Catalog. They can now draw their own walk from the marked hiking network and rank Catalog DayHikes against it. That needs a graph of the whole marked network, which the Catalog and the GeometryStore do not hold: they keep the main walk of each surviving DayHike, not the relations ingest dropped, and not how the ways of different relations meet.

**The TrailGraph is a sidecar written from the work file.** `build_catalog` writes `{catalog}.trailgraph.sqlite` from the ingest work file just before it deletes it, so there is no new OSM pass. Its input is every member way of every `route=hiking` relation ingest read, including relations dropped as too short, too long, superroutes or node-network legs. Unmarked paths stay out, and so do short relations from the Catalog: the 1–20 km band holds, and a short relation appears only as Segments.

**Junctions and Segments.** Ways split at every node two of them share. Two same-layer ways that cross with no node get a Junction at the crossing. A Junction also sits where the set of relations along a trail changes at a node only two ways share, so a stage boundary is a Junction and a Catalog stage can be composed from its own Segments. Otherwise degree-2 chains merge, Junction to Junction. Each Segment keeps its OSM way and relation ids, metres and surface per way, strictest `sac_scale`, and full-resolution vertices (coordinates as OSM's 1e-7 degree integers, so a Segment's vertices are exactly the GeometryStore's), with an R*Tree over Segment boxes.

**Snapping.** A dead-end tip joins the nearest node of another way within the snap tolerance (`ingest --snap-tolerance`, 20 m by default, at most the 50 m `GAP_JOIN_M`) as a `gap` Segment, but only when that node is more than three times the tolerance away along the network, and never across a bridge, a tunnel or another layer. Two ways that pass within the tolerance, with no join nearer than 100 m along the network, are not joined: their closest node pair is offered as a Link ("connect here"), which the Hiker may cross as a straight step.

**Per-vertex samples.** `sample-trailgraph` stores per vertex what the Measures read from rasters: FABDEM height, WorldCover class, CHELSA cell (the cell's 12 months × 3 values once in a table), slope southness at the SolarAspect points, and the WorldCover overview cells beside open water near the network. A serving container has no rasters (ADR-0014), so this is what lets it compute a ComposedHike's facts.

**A ComposedHike is measured as drawn.** It is a start Junction and a list of Segment ids, each starting where the walk so far ends. It may reuse a Segment and counts its distance each time it is walked. Its facts come from the same functions as a DayHike's facts, on the vertices walked, and its amenity facts from the amenity points kept near the network, measured from its start. It is not stored: its URL shares it (see below).

**A shared link names OSM data, not rows.** Segment ids are row numbers and a crossing Junction's id follows build order, so both change with every ingest, and on a newer snapshot Junctions come and go too: a new trail touching a Segment splits it, a removed side trail merges two. A link must outlive all of that, so it names no Junction or Segment. It is a Route, `#compose=1:104.11,105.13`: the start node's key, then one step per OSM way walked, `{way}.{node}`, the way id (negative against the way's node order, which tells the two halves of a ring apart) and the key of the node where the walk leaves that way; `g.{node}` crosses a gap and `l.{node}` a Link. A node's key is its OSM node id, or `c{lat}_{lon}` in 1e-7 degrees for a crossing ingest added. Each Segment stores its runs of steps per way (`parts`: way with direction, metres, surface, step count, `sac_scale`), its OSM node ids, and its steps from either end (`step_ab`, `step_ba`, what the page writes); `segment_ways` indexes Segments by way. The resolver follows each step's way from the node reached so far, through any Junctions, to the step's node, wherever it now lies on a Segment, so a walk may start or turn inside a Segment and the stitched vertices are the same as before the rebuild. Where that fails, the link is refused with what changed: the way is off the network, a node is no longer on the way, or the way no longer leads from one node to the other; it never opens a different walk. A ring with no Junction starts at its lowest OSM node id, not at a build-order node. The serving export keeps the node ids for this. The link also carries the name the Hiker gave the hike; without one the server proposes it from the two trails walked longest and the nearest named amenity point within 500 m of the start.

**Ranking against a ComposedHike.** The serving export carries a TrailGraph subset (`trailgraph.sqlite`, export format 4, with each Segment's simplified line and climb each way). `GET /api/segments` serves the Segments in a map box; `POST /api/compose` returns the same detail shape as a Catalog hike; the ranking and pair endpoints and `dayhike compose` accept a composed Seed. A composed Seed is compared with Catalog DayHikes only (ADR-0020 adds SuggestedLoops to every Ranking). The DayHikes it retraces, its Twins on their served lines, join its Twin group and are not listed. Its facts are scored beside the memory-mapped similarity store, not appended to a copy of it, so a composed Ranking or pair costs no more memory than a DayHike's.

**Considered Options**: unmarked paths too (doubles the graph and invites walks no one maintains); a second OSM pass for the graph (the work file already has every way ingest read); auto-joining every pass-by within the tolerance (two trails on either side of a stream would merge; a Link lets the Hiker decide); keeping short relations as DayHikes (they would crowd the Catalog; as Segments they serve composing); precomputing combinations as candidate DayHikes and suggesting composed routes as similar hikes (a later change); storing ComposedHikes on the server (the URL is enough); links of Segment ids (they break or point elsewhere after a rebuild); links of coordinates (they would have to be snapped back to the network, which could pick another trail). Rejected.

**Consequences**: the Catalog must be rebuilt (`ingest`), then `sample-trailgraph`, then the serving export, to compose hikes; an export without a TrailGraph serves as before, without the "design your own" button, and the export refuses a TrailGraph `sample-trailgraph` never ran on when the similarity facts read the rasters. In the composer, a Segment clicked away from the walk's end is reached the shortest way over trail Segments, drawn apart so the Hiker sees what was added, and never over a gap or Link they did not take themselves. The TrailGraph for Slovenia (0.75 M vertices) is about 38 MB beside the Catalog and 38 MB in the serving export, which keeps the OSM node ids, the `segment_ways` index and the longer `parts` and steps that resolve shared links (28 MB in the export before them; measured on that build's file brought to this schema, not a fresh ingest), so a Europe image grows by about 2–2.7 GB rather than the 1.5–2 GB first estimated. A composed hike may be at most 100 km long and walk at most 50,000 vertex steps (500 Segments of Slovenia's mean 72 vertices, with room), checked while its steps are resolved and stitched, so a long back-and-forth link is refused before its facts are computed; a link may hold at most 2,000 steps. Composing a Catalog DayHike's own Segments reproduces its facts; on Slovenia 1,514 of 1,672 do exactly. Of the rest, 46 differ only in Difficulty or SurfaceMix (the Catalog counts a Leftover's `sac_scale`, and overlapping OSM ways credit surface differently), and 112 cannot be drawn Segment by Segment, because their main walk jumps a gap of up to 50 m inside the relation that the TrailGraph does not join, or steps over a repeated node the main walk dropped. WaterSide of a ComposedHike reads the stored cells, which reach as far as the 200 m column at the cell centre's latitude, so it can differ from a raster read at the edge of a cell. A crossing Junction adds a vertex the DayHike lacks, so a walk across it is a few centimetres longer.
