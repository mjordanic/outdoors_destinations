# A DayStage walks its tour's way, and a liked multi-day route is compared one way

A hiker tester found two things wrong with multi-day routes on the Europe and US build of 2026-11-20.

**Stage order.** The Wicklow Way (OSM relation 2740, "Wicklow Way near Clonegal", 128 km) starts at Clonegal and walks north. Its stages read "stage 4: Glendalough – Pier Gates Car Park", "stage 5: Curtlestown Wood – Pier Gates Car Park", "stage 6: to Curtlestown Wood". Stage 4 climbs from 134 m to 448 m, so it kept the tour's way. Stages 5 and 6 walk down the tour from 448 m to 249 m and from 249 m to 85 m. Each pair of ends differs by over 100 m, so ADR-0048's height rule started each one at its lower end and swapped its name. Stage k + 1 then no longer started where stage k ended, and the list read out of order.

**The way back of a tour.** Return to start is on by default, so a liked multi-day route was measured with its WayBack. The tester saw "201 km one way (402 km with the way back)" and "237 km with the way back" for treks nobody walks back. A Ranking from such a route compared every hike up and back too.

**A DayStage walks its tour's way.** The stages are cut from the tour's main walk in walking order (ADR-0040). Each stage now keeps that direction whatever its own heights, so stage k + 1 starts where stage k ends, and its name runs from its start to its end. The tour itself still starts by ADR-0048's rules: tags, then height, then the rest. A DayStage that walks down its tour starts at its upper end. That is the tour's direction, and it is how someone walking the tour walks the stage. ADR-0048's "a flipped stage keeps its number in the tour and swaps its name's ends" no longer applies. NetworkHikes and other built lines keep the height rule. External hikes' stages already walked their line's way. The Twin check is direction-free, so the same stages are left out as before. This is an ingest change. It takes effect on the next rebuild, and every DayStage that the height rule flipped (5,099 on rebuild 3) then starts at its other end, with its climb and descent swapped.

**A liked multi-day route is measured one way.** The page asks for the liked hike with its way back when Return to start is ticked. If the answer is a multi-day route (`multi_day`, longer than 40 km one way, ADR-0056), the page asks again one way. A composed hike gets the same treatment. The page already takes a Ranking's and a pair's mode from the liked hike, so both are then one way as well. The panel says "A multi-day route is compared one way: Return to start adds no way back to a tour, and the hikes compared with it are measured one way too." The Ranking says "each one way, as a multi-day route is compared" and shows no "One way, without the way back" filter chip. Ticking or unticking Return to start then measures nothing again: only the address changes. The Round trip weight is used, as in any one-way comparison. A Wish keeps Return to start as ticked. A multi-day route listed in a day hike's Ranking with the way back keeps it too: the two modes are never mixed in one comparison (ADR-0019), and such a route is listed after the day hikes anyway (ADR-0056). The API is unchanged: `return_to_start` still means what it says on every endpoint. This is a page change, live as soon as the page is served.

**Considered Options.** Name a flipped stage's ends in tour order (rejected: the name would then run against the line's start, its profile and its GPX). Number the stages again after they are flipped (rejected: the numbers would no longer follow the tour). Make the server ignore `return_to_start` for every multi-day hike (rejected: a multi-day candidate opened from a Ranking with the way back would be shown one way beside scores made with it). Hide the way back only in a multi-day panel's text (rejected: the facts table and the Ranking would still count it).

**Also checked, and correct as is.**
- *Gjendesheim-Besseggen-Memurubu* (relation 1417687) is 7.6 km. The relation leaves out the 154 m way 1417738777 between its ways 1417738775 and 1417738776. That gap is wider than the 50 m (`GAP_JOIN_M`) the main walk joins, so the walk stops there, and the other 6.6 km is counted as unconnected (ADR-0012). The two pieces add up to the ~14 km of the guidebooks. The fix belongs in OpenStreetMap: add that way to the relation.
- *Tour du Combeynot en 4 jours* (external hike 581891012164, 42 km) is cut into 2 stages of 22 and 20 km. Stages are cut at day lengths of 8 to 25 km, as near 16 km as the huts, places and parking along the line allow (ADR-0040). The source's own day count plays no part.
- *Beara Way*: the external "Beara Way near Eyeries" (1066805661180, Sport Ireland trail/3, 73 km) keeps one stage, "Beara Way, stage 4: from Drombane" (553087686378). 87 % of its line lies on the OSM tour (relation 2989585), along that tour's stages 3 to 6. Its stages there are Twins of those and are left out (ADR-0062). Only 35 % of its stage 4 lies on the OSM line, so that stage is kept. It is listed next to the OSM tour's stages 1 to 10 (relation 2989585) because both tours are named "Beara Way" before ADR-0068 tells their labels apart.
