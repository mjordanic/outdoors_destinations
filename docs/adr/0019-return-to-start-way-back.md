# Return to start: every hike measured with its way back

A Hiker who parks at the Trailhead walks back to the car, but the Catalog stores a line one way (ADR-0007, ADR-0012): a 6 km walk up to a summit counted as 6 km and its climb, against a 12 km loop with the same climb. ReturnToStart, on by default, measures every hike of a query with its WayBack, the Seed (a DayHike or a ComposedHike) and every DayHike compared with it alike, so a summit line is compared up and back. Off, every hike is compared one way, as before. The two modes are never mixed: Seed and candidates always use the same one.

**The WayBack is the shortest walk back, not a copy of the hike.** From the hike's last vertex to its first: nothing when they are the same point; a straight step when they are at most 50 m apart; else the shortest walk over the TrailGraph's trail and gap Segments (never a Link, which may be two trails that do not meet) when it is shorter than retracing the hike; else the hike retraced. There is no cut-off that turns a near-loop into a retrace: a hike that ends 500 m from its start adds about 500 m. A line usually adds its own retrace (twice the length; its climb and its descent both ways), but a shorter way back is taken where the network has one.

**Ends within 50 m take a straight step, on purpose.** 50 m (`GAP_JOIN_M`) is the Catalog's own RoundTrip rule (a DayHike whose main walk ends within 50 m of its Trailhead is a RoundTrip) and the gap the main walk itself joins between its ways. Two ends a few metres apart are the same place for a Hiker; a search over the network could send that last step on a long detour (two trail ends on either side of a stream, or ends that are not on the TrailGraph), and a retrace would double the hike.

**No path back: retrace the hike.** When the TrailGraph has no shorter way, when an end is not a TrailGraph vertex, or when there is no TrailGraph, the hike walks back the way it came. That is always possible (the hike itself was walked) and is what a Hiker does on a line. A retrace or a step walks no new ground, so it adds no climate: the hike's own mean climate stands. A trail way back adds its vertices' samples from `sample-trailgraph` to the facts, as a ComposedHike's are computed.

**A second set of facts with its own Calibration.** `build-similarity` measures every Catalog DayHike twice: one way, as before, and with its WayBack, from the same functions on the DayHike's vertices followed by the way back's (length adds the metres back; highest, lowest and Difficulty take the way back's too; LandCover counts every vertex walked; climate averages the way back's cells with the DayHike's own, weighted by vertex count). The second set is calibrated on its own random pairs, because with the way back a line's length doubles and every hike is a RoundTrip, so raw scores mean something else. It is stored beside the first (`facts_back`, `profile_back`, `quantiles_back`) with `way_back` (metres back, climb and descent of the whole walk, and kind), and Twins stay those of the one-way walks. A ComposedHike Seed is measured the same way at query time, its way back found on the served TrailGraph. The detail of a hike in this mode shows the facts, climb and descent with the way back; its line and ElevationProfile stay the hike's own.

**Considered Options**: double every line (a hike ending 100 m from its start would be compared as twice its length); a tolerance under which a near-loop counts as closed (the gap is real walking, and any cut-off flips at its edge); a straight line home for every hike (it crosses valleys and ridges); computing the way back at query time for every candidate (a graph search per DayHike per query); one Calibration for both modes (random pairs of round trips score length and the RoundTrip Measure differently). Rejected.

**Consequences**: `build-similarity` reads the TrailGraph and its samples, so `sample-trailgraph` runs before it and now deletes the similarity facts, as every sample step does; the facts must be rebuilt, and the serving export after them. The similarity directory and the export about double (the facts and Calibration are stored twice). `build-similarity` measures every DayHike twice and searches the TrailGraph once per DayHike; the search stops at the best way back found so far (at most the hike's own length), so its work grows with the trails within that distance, not with the whole TrailGraph. With the way back every hike ends at its start, so the `round_trip` Measure agrees on every pair and calibrates to 0.5. Default Rankings change: a line now compares with its up-and-back. The Python `rank_dayhikes`, the API, the page and the CLI default to the way back; `return_to_start=False` (`--one-way`, `&oneway` in the page's address) compares one way.
