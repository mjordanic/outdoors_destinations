# Core Measures by default, WaterSide, Twins, and the similarity as the score

Blind judges rated the web demo's top 10 for 17 Seeds against their own reading of each DayHike (facts, map, absolute-altitude profile). Ranking was broadly sensible, but five gaps were systematic: no coast or lake signal; amenity Measures that agree on absence gave every pair 0.57–0.75 for free; `highest` and `lowest` forgave 1,000 m; duplicate relations and reversed stages took top-10 slots; and the shown score, a percentile among 20,000 random pairs, read 1.000 for 28 of 170 results. ADR-0013 stays (calibrated Measures, a weighted mean, the store, `rank_dayhikes`); this changes five things in it.

**WaterSide.** A 27th Measure, `waterside`, compares the share of each walk within 200 m of open water (tolerance, D = 0.5). A probe every 200 m along the walk counts when at least a tenth of the WorldCover cells within 200 m of it are permanent water, which is sea, lakes and wide rivers alike. `build-similarity` reads the WorldCover tiles already fetched for LandCover, at 4 × 10 m cells through the tiles' overviews (a few seconds per tile), so there is no new data source and no ingest change. OSM coastline and water polygons were not ingested; adding them would mean a new Europe-wide ingest pass.

**Core Measures by default.** With no weights, eleven Measures weigh 100 and the other sixteen are off: `length`, `elevation_gain`, `highest`, `lowest`, `profile`, `landcover`, `temperature`, `precipitation`, `solar_radiation`, `climb_grade`, and `waterside`. The first ten are the ones that separated well-rated from poorly rated results for the blind judges, and re-averaging over them raised agreement with the judges from Spearman 0.31 to 0.46; the facility flags, `peak_summited`, `viewpoint_along`, `water_count`, `dry_stretch`, `round_trip`, `tarmac_approach`, `surface`, `steepest_km`, `southness`, `difficulty` and `peaks_around` showed no separation, or depend on how densely OSM is mapped. They stay available at any weight. The web page starts its weights panel from the same defaults.

**Altitude tolerance.** `highest` and `lowest` reach 0 at 400 m apart instead of 1,000 m, so a fjord-side walk 500 m lower than an alpine Seed, or a sea-level loop against a valley walk 1,000 m up, no longer matches on altitude.

**Twins.** Two DayHikes are Twins when at least 80 % of each one's walk (sampled every 50 m) lies within 30 m of the other's line, so a route and its reverse, or two OSM relations of one trail, are Twins, while a variant that shares less, or a short walk inside a long one, is not. Twins of Twins form one group. `build-similarity` finds them once from the GeometryStore (only pairs whose lengths and point centres allow 80 % are compared) and stores each DayHike's group as `twins.npy`. Ranking keeps the best of each group and drops the Twins of a Seed, after scoring and before `limit`.

**The score is the similarity.** The shown score is the similarity itself, the weighted mean of the calibrated Measure scores of the best Seed. It reads like the per-Measure cells it averages: a random pair scores about 0.5 on average, and 1.0 needs a pair better than every random pair on every weighted Measure, so the top results no longer tie. The 20,000 stored random pairs (`pairs.npy`) existed only for the percentile and are gone.

**Considered Options**: OSM coastline and water polygons for WaterSide (needs a new ingest pass over the Europe PBF); distance to the nearest water instead of a share of the walk (one cove makes a mountain walk coastal); keep all 27 Measures at 100 and only lower the amenity group (the judges' evidence favours the core set; the others remain one click away); calibrate the altitude tolerance from the population (the judges' reading of 400 m is the evidence we have; a population fit answers a different question); group Twins at query time from the serving geometry (the CLI ranks without it, ADR-0006); a larger random-pair sample or an extrapolated tail for the percentile (any percentile saturates for the best of 161,000 candidates); rescale the score over the shown ten (the same hike would score differently in another query). Rejected.

**Consequences**: the facts must be rebuilt (`build-similarity`, which now also needs `--landcover`), and the serving export after them. A DayHike off every WorldCover tile has no WaterSide, and the Measure drops out as any missing fact does. A score near 0.5 is a typical pair, not "half alike"; unrelated pairs score about 0.2–0.4 instead of near 0. The golden rankings of the rebuild checks were recorded with the old score and constants and must be recaptured.
